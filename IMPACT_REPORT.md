Social Return on Investment (SROI) measures the social value created per rupee invested. For CovA 126:

### Inputs (Year 1)

|---|---|

| Platform co-contributions | ₹1.6 Cr |

### Social Outputs (Year 1)

| Social Output | Monetised Value | Methodology |
|---|---|---|
| Direct income protection paid out | ₹10.2 Cr | Claim data |
| Informal debt cycles prevented (₹8,000 avg. high-interest loan) | ₹4.96 Cr | 6,200 × ₹8,000 |

**SROI Ratio = Social Value ÷ Investment = ₹20.96 Cr ÷ ₹26.2 Cr = 0.80**

> For every ₹1 invested in CovA 126 (premiums + platform contribution + technology), ₹0.80 in measurable social value is created in Year 1, rising to **₹1.24 SROI by Year 3** as scale reduces per-worker technology cost.

A SROI > 1.0 (Year 3 target) means the social value created exceeds the total investment — the platform creates more value than it costs to run.

## 4. The Gig Worker Financial Inclusion Effect

### 4.1 The Insurance Paradox for Gig Workers

India's financial inclusion drive (PM Jan Dhan Yojana, PM Suraksha Bima Yojana, PM Jeevan Jyoti) has successfully brought bank accounts and basic life insurance to low-income households. But gig workers fall through a structural gap:

| Product | Monthly Cost | Works for Gig Workers? | Why Not |
|---|---|---|---|
| PM Suraksha Bima | ₹20/month | Partial | Accident only, not income loss |
| PM Jeevan Jyoti | ₹436/year (upfront) | No | Annual payment impossible on irregular income |
| Digit Pay-Per-Day | ₹270–₹870/month | No | Health/accident, not income, manual claim |
| Bajaj Allianz Smart Micro | Monthly | No | Monthly premium, health only |
| **CovA 126** | **₹28–₹72/week** | **YES** | Income-specific, weekly, automated |

### 4.2 CovA 126 as Financial Inclusion Infrastructure

CovA 126 extends India's financial inclusion stack into the gig economy by solving two problems simultaneously:

**Problem 1: Premium Affordability** — ₹47/week = ₹6.71/day. A Q-commerce worker earning ₹820/day pays 0.82% of their daily income for coverage. Below any financial burden threshold.

**Problem 2: Claims Accessibility** — Traditional insurance requires documentation a gig worker cannot produce during a disruption. Parametric insurance eliminates documentation entirely. The trigger data IS the proof.

## 5. Regulatory Alignment — Tailwinds, Not Headwinds

CovA 126 is structurally aligned with every major Indian insurance regulatory development from 2020–2026:

### 5.1 Social Security Code 2020

The Code mandates that gig-platform aggregators (Zepto, Blinkit, Swiggy Instamart) contribute to social security for registered gig workers. Section 114 explicitly includes "platform workers" in the definition of eligible beneficiaries for social security schemes.

**CovA 126 alignment:** The platform co-contribution model (₹4/worker/week from the Q-commerce platform) is directly structured as the platform's Social Security Code contribution channel. Platforms fulfil their regulatory obligation through CovA 126, rather than creating a separate compliance mechanism.

### 5.2 IRDAI Micro-Insurance Regulations 2023

The 2023 regulations explicitly:
- Encourage parametric products for low-income beneficiaries
- Permit simplified KYC and documentation requirements for micro-insurance
- Allow premium collection frequencies below monthly (including weekly)
- Permit digital-only distribution without physical agent intermediaries

**CovA 126 alignment:** Every design decision in CovA 126 — weekly premiums, parametric triggers, digital-only onboarding, zero documentation — is explicitly sanctioned by the 2023 regulations. We are not seeking regulatory exemptions. We are the reference implementation of what the regulator said it wanted.

### 5.3 IRDAI Regulatory Sandbox (Cohort 6/7)

The IRDAI Regulatory Sandbox program allows innovative insurance products to operate under relaxed licensing requirements for up to 24 months. CovA 126's parametric income-protection model qualifies under Cohort 5's precedents (agricultural parametric insurance approvals).

**Roadmap:** IRDAI Sandbox application in Q4 2026, targeting Cohort 7 (est. Q1 2027 opening). Sandbox approval provides 24 months of commercial operation with relaxed solvency margin requirements — reducing capital requirements by ~60% during the scaling phase.

### 5.4 India's ONDC (Open Network for Digital Commerce)

As ONDC expands to logistics and last-mile delivery, Q-commerce platforms will be required to expose standardised shift-start/stop webhooks for regulatory compliance. This is CovA 126's primary CDI trigger source in the production architecture.

**Implication:** The regulatory infrastructure (ONDC webhooks) for parametric income protection will be mandated by the government — reducing CovA 126's data acquisition costs and eliminating the need for individual platform API negotiations at scale.

## 8. The Structural Change

CovA 126 does not ask whether gig workers deserve income protection. That question was settled.

It solves the three engineering problems that made income protection economically impossible to deliver:

1. **LAE was 5× higher than the claim value** → TCHC eliminates it to ₹0 via hardware validation and Master Payload batch processing.
2. **GPS fraud made automated payouts commercially suicidal** → TCHC's 3-modal physical validation is undefeatable by software-layer spoofing.
3. **Weekly income cycles made monthly/annual premiums unaffordable** → CPR computes a weekly premium that costs < 1% of daily income.

Three engineering solutions. One structural change. 2.3 million workers who go to work tomorrow slightly safer than they did today.
